VIEWER FUNCTIONS The viewer displays either a procedurally generated alpine terrain or an imported PLY, STL, or OBJ model. In "Example Terrain" a terrain model from Kandersteg can be loaded. The camera can be rotated, zoomed, and panned using OrbitControls. SHIFT + LEFT CLICK moves the source area. CMD or CTRL + LEFT CLICK adds points to a custom polygonal release area. Press ENTER to finish drawing the polygon. TERRAIN REPRESENTATION The terrain is represented as a regular heightfield. Each horizontal position has one terrain height and one interpolated terrain normal. This makes collision calculations efficient, but means that overhangs, caves, vertical walls, bridges, and multiple terrain layers are not represented by the physics. MACRO-PARTICLES The material volume is divided into macro-particles using: source volume × particles per m³. Each particle represents a volume parcel rather than one physical grain. With the default settings, 7,000 m³ and approximately 1,000 particles result in approximately 7 m³ per particle. A global particle mass is not used independently because all particles represent equal-volume parcels. MATERIAL PARAMETERS The material model uses particle friction, terrain friction, static friction, cohesion, contact damping, and velocity- dependent flow drag. The defaults are tuned for a visually coherent flow rather than for calibrated prediction of a specific real event. GRAVITY AND TERRAIN SLOPE Particles above the terrain receive ordinary vertical gravity. Particles close to the terrain receive gravity projected onto the tangent plane of the terrain. This produces downhill acceleration. FRICTION AND CONTACT Static friction prevents slow particles from creeping on gentle slopes. Moving particles use kinetic friction. Nearby particles are found with a spatial hash. Overlapping particles are pushed apart. Relative normal velocity is corrected, tangential contact motion is reduced, and contact damping reduces jitter. COHESION Nearby particles are attracted only when they are farther apart than a preferred rest distance. Particles already in contact are not additionally attracted. This reduces excessive clumping while preserving a coherent flow appearance. FLOW DRAG A quadratic drag force is applied to the tangential velocity near the terrain. Drag increases with the square of velocity and limits unrealistically high speeds. EROSION AND DEPOSITION Fast-moving particles in contact with sufficiently inclined terrain erode a small amount of terrain. Particles that have travelled and later come to rest deposit a small amount of material. The terrain is updated periodically rather than after every particle. The process is a visual heightfield approximation and is not a fully mass-conservative sediment budget. CHANGE HIGHLIGHTING When enabled, accumulated erosion is shown in red and accumulated deposition is shown in blue. The colors describe the history of terrain change, not the current particle velocity. COLOUR MODES VELOCITY colours particles according to their current speed. DISTANCE TRAVELED colours particles according to accumulated travel distance. FLOW THICKNESS estimates local volume per terrain cell and colours particles according to local thickness. WHAT THIS IS NOT This is not an MPM solver, FLIP solver, PIC solver, SPH solver, or complete DEM solver. It does not solve pressure, fluid incompressibility, stress tensors, pore-water pressure, angular particle rotation, conservative sediment transport, or full material constitutive laws. RELIABILITY The simulation is intended for qualitative visualisation, design exploration, and comparison of parameter settings. It should not be used for engineering design, structural loading, legal analysis, hazard prediction, or quantitative forecasting of a real debris-flow event.
