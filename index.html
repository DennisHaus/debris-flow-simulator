Debris Flow Simulator – Read Me

Overview

The Debris Flow Simulator is a simplified terrain-based model for observing sediment movement across changing topography.

It is intended for visualisation, design exploration, and comparison of parameter settings. It must not be used for engineering design, hazard prediction, legal analysis, or quantitative forecasting of a real debris-flow event.

Material and flow

The simulator uses macro-particles. Each macro-particle represents a volume parcel rather than an individual physical grain.

  • Particle–terrain friction: Resistance between the moving material and the terrain surface.
  • Particle–particle friction: Resistance to sliding during particle contact.
  • Particle cohesion: Attraction between particles that move apart from their internal rest distance.
  • Start velocity: Initial velocity assigned to the released material.
  • Simulation speed: Playback multiplier for the simulation.

Numerical stabilisation values such as terrain drag, collision damping, static support, cohesion distance, and cohesion range are kept internal to the model. They are not presented as independent physical material parameters.

Terrain evolution

When enabled, fast-moving particles can erode terrain while particles that have travelled and later come to rest can deposit material.

Terrain changes are applied periodically to a regular heightfield. The result is a visual approximation rather than a calibrated, mass-conservative sediment transport model.

Change overlay

The change overlay compares the current terrain with the terrain stored at the beginning of the simulation.

  • Blue: Deposition, where the terrain has gained height.
  • Red: Erosion, where the terrain has lost height.

The display scale controls the terrain-height difference represented by full colour intensity.

Interaction

  • Shift + left click: Move the release source across the terrain while paused.
  • Play: Start or resume the simulation.
  • Reset: Restore the source, terrain, particles, and simulation time.
  • New Terrain: Generate a new procedural terrain.
  • Example Terrain: Load the example Kandersteg terrain.
  • Choose 3D Model: Import a PLY, STL, or OBJ terrain model.

Terrain representation

The terrain is represented as a regular heightfield. Each horizontal position has one terrain height and one interpolated terrain normal.

Overhangs, caves, vertical walls, bridges, and multiple terrain layers are not represented by this model.

Model limitations

This simulator is not an MPM, FLIP, PIC, SPH, or complete DEM solver. It does not solve pressure, incompressibility, pore-water pressure, stress tensors, angular particle rotation, or a complete sediment budget.

Gravity, friction, cohesion, particle contact, erosion, and deposition are simplified for visual and exploratory purposes.
