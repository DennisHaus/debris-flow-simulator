A simplified terrain-based model for observing sediment movement across changing topography. – For visualisation purposes only.

Developed by Dennis Häusler, Institute for Landscape and Urban Studies, ETH Zurich, 2026, haeusler@arch.ethz.ch
assisted by AI (GPT-5.6)
DETAILED DESCRIPTION VIEWER FUNCTIONS The viewer displays either a procedurally generated alpine terrain or an imported PLY, STL, or OBJ model. The camera can be rotated, zoomed, and panned using OrbitControls. SHIFT + LEFT CLICK moves the source area. CMD or CTRL + LEFT CLICK adds points to a custom polygonal release area. Press ENTER to finish drawing the polygon. TERRAIN REPRESENTATION The terrain is represented as a regular heightfield. Every horizontal position has one terrain height and one interpolated terrain normal. This makes collision calculations efficient, but means that overhangs, caves, vertical walls, bridges, and multiple terrain layers are not represented by the physics. MACRO-PARTICLES The material volume is divided into macro-particles using: source volume × particles per m³. Each particle represents a volume parcel rather than one physical grain. With the default settings, 7,000 m³ and approximately 1,000 particles result in approximately 7 m³ per particle. A spherical equivalent radius is calculated from this parcel volume. The particle mass is not used as an independent parameter because all particles represent equal-volume parcels. GRAVITY AND TERRAIN SLOPE Particles above the terrain receive ordinary vertical gravity. Particles close to the terrain receive gravity projected onto the tangent plane of the terrain. This produces explicit downhill acceleration. STATIC AND KINETIC FRICTION Static friction prevents slow particles from creeping on gentle terrain slopes. Once a particle is moving, kinetic friction reduces its tangential velocity. This creates a distinction between resting and sliding material. FLOW DRAG A velocity-dependent quadratic drag is applied to particles close to the terrain. Drag increases with the square of tangential velocity and limits unrealistically high speeds. PARTICLE CONTACT Nearby particles are found with a spatial hash. Overlapping particles are pushed apart. Relative normal velocity is corrected, tangential contact motion is reduced by material friction, and contact damping reduces jitter. COHESION Nearby particles are attracted only when they are farther apart than a preferred rest distance. Particles that are already in contact are not additionally attracted. This reduces artificial clumping while keeping the material visually coherent. EROSION AND DEPOSITION The optional terrain-evolution model changes the terrain heightfield over time. Fast-moving particles in contact with sufficiently inclined terrain erode a small amount of terrain. Particles that have travelled and later come to rest deposit a small amount of material. Terrain changes are accumulated during physics steps and applied periodically. The terrain mesh and physics normals are then updated together. COLOUR MODES VELOCITY colours particles according to their current speed. DISTANCE TRAVELED colours particles according to accumulated travel distance. FLOW THICKNESS estimates local volume per terrain cell and colours particles according to that local thickness. SIMULATION CACHE Periodic copies of particle positions, velocities, travelled distances, deposition state, and terrain heights are stored in memory. The timeline restores previously recorded states. It does not calculate the simulation backward. WHAT THIS IS NOT This is not an MPM solver, FLIP solver, PIC solver, SPH solver, or complete DEM solver. It does not solve pressure, fluid incompressibility, stress tensors, pore-water pressure, angular particle rotation, conservative sediment transport, or full material constitutive laws. RELIABILITY The simulation is intended for qualitative visualisation, design exploration, and comparison of parameter settings. It should not be used for engineering design, structural loading, legal analysis, hazard prediction, or quantitative forecasting of a real debris-flow event.
